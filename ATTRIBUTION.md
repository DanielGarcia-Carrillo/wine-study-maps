# Source attribution

The data retain their own source-specific terms. Geometry JSON is transformed upstream data, not solely original project artwork. See `data/input-manifest.json` for each actual input, checksum, transformation and affected output.

## Base geography and coordinates

Every `wset-map-*` file is generated by `scripts/render_maps.py`.
Region geometry comes from source data; label anchors and layout may be set
explicitly in `catalogue.yaml`. The rendered maps simplify those inputs for
illustration and are not official, legal or surveyed boundaries.

**Italy.** Piedmont publishes the boundaries of its DOC and DOCG production
areas. Veneto and Tuscany publish none, so their DOCs and DOCGs come from the
EU PDO inventory described under Spain below. The Piedmont file is a shapefile in UTM zone 32N,
so it is reprojected to WGS84 by `utm_to_wgs84()`. The automated suite
checks the UTM 32N exact origin. No independent Piedmont town containment
check is currently implemented; the processed boundary inputs are checksummed.

**Spain, and the rest of the EU.** The DOs are drawn from the EU wine PDO
inventory of Candiago et al. (2022), built from the EU register's own
delimitations and published CC0. The Spanish ministry publishes the same
zones, but only behind a download form. The same file supplies Valpolicella,
Soave, Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, the Mosel, Rheingau and Pfalz, the
Douro and Tokaj. It is not infallible: its Chianti Classico lists 76 comuni
where the disciplinare names nine, so Chianti Classico is drawn from Vin Santo
del Chianti Classico instead, whose disciplinare takes the whole Chianti
Classico territory. The inventory works in whole comuni, and four of those
eight lie only partly in the zone, so that shape runs somewhat large. The GeoPackage is in EPSG:3035 (LAEA Europe), reprojected by
`laea_to_wgs84()`, using the EPSG guidance note's inverse formulas. Tests
check the projection origin and selected town coordinates from independent sources: Haro inside Rioja, Aranda de Duero inside Ribera del Duero and
Gratallops inside Priorat, Bernkastel-Kues inside the Mosel, Neustadt inside
the Pfalz, Montalcino inside Brunello di Montalcino and Tokaj inside Tokaj.
Catalunya stays the administrative region, since the catalogue uses it as one.

**Australia.** Wine Australia GI boundaries are an interpretation of the legal text; the legal text prevails. Hunter Valley is registered as "Hunter".

**Chile.** Decreto 464 defines every Chilean wine DO by administrative units,
so the units are the boundary: the Central Valley region is the Región
Metropolitana, O'Higgins and Maule; Maipo Valley is the Región Metropolitana;
Colchagua Valley is the provinces of Colchagua and Cardenal Caro; Casablanca
Valley is the comuna of Casablanca. The provinces and the comuna come from
OpenStreetMap as administrative units, which is the one use of OSM allowed
here.

**South Africa.** No open Wine of Origin boundary data was found (SAWIS
publishes ward boundaries only as prose notices), so Stellenbosch,
Constantia, Elgin and Walker Bay stay marked inside the Western Cape.

**How the French appellation areas are built.** INAO's own note on the dataset
says the official area of an AOC is expressed *"sous forme de liste de
communes"*. So each appellation here is drawn as the union of the commune
boundaries INAO lists for it. This follows the commune-list definition of
the geographic area; the rendered illustration simplifies the geometry and
is not a legal boundary document. Where the table names a commune whose INSEE code has
since been retired by a merger, the successor commune is normally listed too,
so the area is unaffected; `source_geodata.py` reports the count either way.

**What is not claimed.** A few regions have no delimited boundary to draw
(Pays d'Oc is an IGP; California's Central Valley is not an AVA; "south-west
France" is represented using the Cahors locator coordinate,
Wikidata `Q23047`, rather than a delimited south-west France polygon). Those are marked with a dot and a scale
halo, which indicates position and rough size only and is **not** a boundary.

**Outlines are computed, not traced.** Where a map outlines an area - the
Côte de Nuits, the Northern Rhône, Crozes-Hermitage - the line is the outer
boundary of the same sourced polygons that are filled: edges shared by two
communes cancel, and what is left is the union's edge. A label `anchor:` is
the one hand-typed coordinate in `catalogue.yaml`, and `source_geodata.py` checks
each one lies inside its own sourced area.

Appellation boundaries were deliberately **not** taken from OpenStreetMap. Its
objects are administrative rather than viticultural, so they are wrong in ways
that are hard to spot: the relation tagged `Médoc` is the *peninsula*
(`Q1435049`), `Saint-Emilion` is the single *commune* while the AOC covers
eight, and Barossa Valley has no region object at all.

**Saint-Emilion.** The current recipe uses its INAO appellation area and has
no Wikidata pin. It does not use a Wikidata coordinate to place that region.
